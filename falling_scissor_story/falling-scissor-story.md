# The Falling Scissor

A single scissor hangs from a thread somewhere above the page. As the visitor scrolls down
past the hero, it falls into view — not instantly, but the way something on a string actually
falls, gathering speed. It reaches the ticket's perforation line and starts to cut, blades
tracking along the dotted tear-line as the scroll continues, the ticket beginning to separate
underneath it.

But the thread was never long enough to finish the job. Mid-cut, it runs out of slack. The
fall stops dead — not a smooth deceleration, a real jerk, the kind of sudden stop a taut
string actually produces. All that arrested downward momentum has to go somewhere, so it goes
sideways: the scissor swings out on its thread like a pendulum let loose, arcs back, swings
again a little smaller, again smaller still, and settles into a faint, slowing sway — hanging
there, mid-air, half a cut behind.

Later, if the visitor scrolls back up, the moment reverses: the thread draws taut again and
pulls the scissor back up out of frame, and as it goes the perforation line closes behind it —
the ticket made whole again, the cut undone.
